Results Summary: SCN5A and Brugada Syndrome
1. Study overview
This laboratory investigation examined how a nucleotide change in the human SCN5A gene may alter the encoded cardiac sodium-channel protein and contribute to the Brugada syndrome phenotype. The wild-type (WT) SCN5A coding sequence was compared with a sequence containing the documented splice-donor variant NM_000335.5:c.4296+1G>A. A separate artificial single-nucleotide substitution was also introduced to demonstrate that a nucleotide change does not necessarily alter the amino-acid sequence.

SCN5A encodes Nav1.5, the principal voltage-gated sodium-channel alpha subunit in cardiac muscle. Nav1.5 supports the rapid sodium influx required for cardiac action-potential initiation and propagation. Reduced or abnormal Nav1.5 function can impair cardiac electrical conduction and increase susceptibility to ventricular arrhythmias associated with Brugada syndrome.

3. Documented mutation examined
The documented variant analyzed in the sequence experiment was SCN5A NM_000335.5:c.4296+1G>A. This is a one-base G-to-A substitution at the +1 position of a splice-donor site. Because the affected nucleotide is part of an intron-exon boundary rather than an ordinary coding codon, its primary predicted effect is abnormal pre-mRNA splicing rather than a direct missense substitution.

4. Computational mutant-sequence results
The mutant sequence generated for the documented variant was translated in the same reading frame as the WT sequence. The translated representation showed a downstream change in the amino-acid sequence followed by premature stop codons. These findings are consistent with a major alteration of the coding sequence after abnormal RNA processing, although sequence translation alone cannot determine the exact transcript produced in human cells.

The analysis therefore supports a change extending beyond a single amino-acid substitution. The most likely biological explanation is that disruption of the splice donor alters the mature SCN5A mRNA, which can change the downstream coding sequence and introduce an early stop codon. The abnormal transcript may also be targeted for nonsense-mediated decay, resulting in little or no mutant protein.

8. Molecular interpretation
The proposed molecular pathway is:

SCN5A c.4296+1G>A
→ disruption of the splice-donor site
→ abnormal SCN5A pre-mRNA processing
→ altered mature coding sequence
→ premature stop codons or nonsense-mediated decay
→ reduced or abnormal Nav1.5 protein
→ reduced cardiac sodium current
→ impaired cardiac conduction
→ increased susceptibility to ventricular arrhythmias and the Brugada syndrome phenotype.

The sequence results support the predicted downstream disruption because the supplied mutant translation contains extensive amino-acid changes and premature stop codons. The biological interpretation is that the variant may reduce the amount of functional Nav1.5 available in cardiomyocyte membranes. Because Nav1.5 is required for the rapid depolarization and propagation of electrical impulses in the heart, reduced channel function can contribute to conduction abnormalities and arrhythmia susceptibility.

9. Results supported directly by the analysis
The following conclusions are directly supported by the sequence and translation comparisons performed in the laboratory exercise:

The documented and WT sequence representations differ downstream of the reported variant region.
The documented mutant representation contains multiple downstream amino-acid changes.
Premature stop codons are present in the translated mutant representation.
The artificial A143T substitution changes the nucleotide sequence but preserves arginine at the protein level.
The artificial mutation does not alter the reading frame or the predicted protein length in the supplied sequence.
The following conclusions require experimental confirmation and should be presented as predictions rather than direct observations:

The exact abnormal SCN5A RNA-splicing product.
Whether the mutant transcript undergoes nonsense-mediated decay.
Whether a truncated Nav1.5 protein is produced and remains stable.
The amount and membrane localization of mutant Nav1.5 protein.
The effect of the variant on sodium current and cardiac action potentials.
The exact relationship between the variant and arrhythmia risk in an individual.
10. Data-consistency note for the GitHub submission
The supplied laboratory draft contains two different variants in the documented-mutation sections. One table lists NM_000335.5:c.481G>A, p.Glu161Lys (E161K), while the sequence experiment and molecular-mechanism sections analyze NM_000335.5:c.4296+1G>A, a splice-donor variant. The supplied citation link also refers to the c.4296+1G>A variant, whereas the earlier table lists a different ClinVar variation/accession.

For a consistent GitHub submission, this results summary treats c.4296+1G>A as the analyzed documented mutation because it is the variant used in the mutant-sequence, translation, and molecular-mechanism sections. Before final submission, the student should confirm the intended ClinVar record and remove or separately label the c.481G>A/E161K entry. The reported first amino-acid difference also appears as approximately position 1,373 in one section and position 1,433 in another; the final report should use the coordinate produced by the final alignment or translation output and explain the software/sequence basis for that coordinate.

11. Conclusion
The sequence analysis illustrates how a single nucleotide can have very different consequences depending on its location. The documented SCN5A c.4296+1G>A variant affects a splice-donor site and is predicted to disrupt RNA processing, causing extensive downstream protein changes and premature termination in the supplied computational representation. This supports a loss-of-function mechanism involving reduced functional Nav1.5 channels and abnormal cardiac electrical conduction. In contrast, the artificial A143T substitution changes a nucleotide but preserves the encoded arginine, demonstrating a synonymous mutation with no direct amino-acid change.

Overall, the experiment connects the molecular levels of disease biology in the following order: gene → nucleotide variant → RNA processing → protein product → cardiac sodium current → electrical conduction → phenotype. The computational results provide a sequence-based prediction, while RNA, protein-expression, and electrophysiological experiments would be needed to confirm the complete disease mechanism.
